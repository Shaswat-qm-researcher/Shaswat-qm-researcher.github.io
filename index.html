01  About me

From heat flow to the quantum physics underneath it

I am a Master's researcher in theoretical and computational physics at Universiti Sains Malaysia, working with Prof. Yoon Tiem Leong on inverse problems in quantum plasmonics: recovering the nonlocal response of the electron gas in gold nanoparticles from their optical spectra with physics-informed neural networks.

Before that, at IIT Kanpur with Prof. Somnath Bhowmick, I worked on lattice dynamics. We derived an interpretable law for the Debye temperature across more than 11,000 inorganic materials, used it to screen for new high-ΘD ceramics, and checked the candidates with first-principles phonon and thermal-transport calculations.

My first degree was in mechanical engineering, where I learned numerical modelling on heat- and mass-transfer problems. I moved to physics to work on the questions underneath them.

Research

In my PhD I want to bring this approach to quantum dynamics and quantum materials.

3.1  M.S. thesis · Universiti Sains Malaysia · 2025 –

Quantum-corrected plasmonics, learned backwards

When a gold particle is only a few nanometres across, its electrons no longer respond locally to light. Can that nonlocal physics be read directly off an optical spectrum?

Eres λres ΔE

Live figure. Dipole plasmon of a gold nanosphere in the local (dashed) and hydrodynamic (solid) models, using the size law from my boundary-element calculations, ΔE = 384.17/R + 4.91 meV, with Γ = 0.165 eV (ħωp = 3.3 eV). Left: the sphere drawn to scale (10 nm bar), with its quasi-static near field and surface charge oscillating at resonance; the charge band has the 0.1 nm cover-layer thickness, so it matters more as the particle shrinks.

384 meVnonlocal blueshift at R = 1 nm
∝ 1/Rblueshift scaling, R² = 0.999
0.01 %RMS error of BEM against Mie theory
≈ 4 fsplasmon lifetime, unchanged by nonlocality
Problem

Below about 10 nm the induced charge is no longer a sharp surface sheet. Electron pressure spreads it over a fraction of a nanometre and shifts the plasmon. Local optics misses this, and full quantum treatments are expensive.

Method

Boundary-element (MNPBEM) simulations of Au nanospheres with R = 1–15 nm, using the hydrodynamic Drude model through a cover-layer mapping, validated against Mie theory. A multi-frequency PINN couples the Helmholtz and hydrodynamic equations, with ωp, γ, β and ε∞ as trainable unknowns.

Findings so far

The blueshift grows as 1/R and reaches 384 meV at 1 nm, while the energy linewidth (Γ ≈ 0.165 eV) stays fixed. A forward PINN, tuned over about 100 Optuna trials, reaches a relative L2 error of about 0.19 against the BEM fields; the inverse retrieval is in progress.

3.2  IIT Kanpur · with Prof. Somnath Bhowmick · 2025

A learned law for the Debye temperature

Can a machine find the governing physics of lattice vibrations, and then use it to discover new materials?

Live figure. The SISSO law ΘD = 150.054 · Vatom−1/3 ρ−1/2 (GVRH + E) / E1/2 − 0.691 (K, with Å³/atom, g cm⁻³, GPa). Presets are compounds from the OQMD screen with their first-principles (phonopy) values. The curve is the Debye heat capacity this ΘD implies; the dots are the phonopy heat capacity of the same compound from my DFT calculations. Phonon DOS compares my DFT phonon density of states (by element) with the Debye model g(ν) ∝ ν² up to νD = kBΘD/h.

View
Regime

Live figure: the latent space. 11,509 inorganic materials from Materials Project and AFLOW, first plotted on the raw (GVRH, ρ) axes and then projected onto the first two principal components of five standardised descriptors (GVRH, E, Vatom, ρ, ΘD), which explain 63.8% and 23.0% of the variance. Arrows are the loadings (×4). Points are coloured by ΘD on a rainbow scale. Classes: the chemical classification of the manuscript, with its marker symbols and the four annotated regions. Pick a critical regime, click a class to isolate it, and hover to identify a compound.

11,509inorganic materials analysed
R² = 0.994on ~4,200 unseen AFLOW compounds
≈ 10 Kmean absolute error
23new stable high-ΘD compounds, 8 above 1000 K
Problem

ΘD sets the energy scale of lattice vibrations: heat capacity, thermal conductivity and the phonon-mediated pairing of conventional superconductors. Computing it from phonons is too costly to do across thousands of materials.

Method

Partial least squares reduced 20+ descriptors to four (GVRH, E, Vatom, ρ). SISSO searched about 26 million candidate expressions. Critical-regime analysis and PCA of 11,509 materials (below) mapped where extreme ΘD occurs, the resulting design rules screened OQMD, and phonopy/phono3py checked the candidates.

Findings

A closed-form law consistent with Anderson's elastic Debye relation, and new high-ΘD compounds that range from high-conductivity ceramics (TiBeC, κL ≈ 75 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹) to rattler compounds with strongly suppressed heat transport.

Next: from ΘD to Tc, connecting these lattice descriptors to superconducting transition temperatures with explainable ML (manuscript in preparation).

3.3  IIT Kanpur · Phys. Rev. B 109, 214102 (2024)

Stacking-fault energies from first principles

Slide one half of an fcc crystal over the other. The energy landscape along the way decides how a metal deforms.

Live figure. The top of a nine-layer fcc stack is sheared along ⟨112⟩ by x partial Burgers vectors, b = a/6⟨112⟩. At x = 1 the stacking reads …ABC|BCA… (intrinsic fault); a second shift of the top three layers gives the extrinsic fault. Curves use the paper's DFT values of γISF, γESF and G, and its fitted constant c for each metal.

Problem

Stacking-fault energies control dislocation dissociation, twinning and ductility in fcc metals, but full generalized stacking-fault curves need many supercell calculations.

Method

DFT supercell and ANNNI calculations for eight fcc metals and three alloys; an analytic GSFE form built from γISF, γESF and the shear modulus; feature selection linking d-band descriptors to SFE and G; a neural-network predictor.

Findings

Fault energies and shear moduli for Ag, Au, Cu, Ni, Pd, Pt, Rh, Ir and their alloys from a few inexpensive calculations, in agreement with experiment.

3.4  IIT Kanpur · 2023

Teaching a network the Schrödinger equation

Find a ground state without solving an eigenvalue problem: make the energy as small as the physics allows.

step ⟨E⟩

Live figure. A random trial wavefunction relaxes to the ground state by descending the energy ⟨ψ|H|ψ⟩/⟨ψ|ψ⟩ (imaginary-time evolution on a grid, ħ = m = 1; inset: energy against step). My PINN/VMCNN solver minimises the same functional with a neural network in place of the grid, for the same three potentials.

Problem

Anharmonic and double-well potentials have no closed-form ground states, and tunnelling spreads the wavefunction across the barrier.

Method

A fully connected network (128-64-32, tanh) represents ψ(x) on Monte-Carlo-sampled points. The loss is the variational energy with Dirichlet and normalisation constraints, minimised with Adam.

Findings

Ground states and energies of harmonic, double-well and asymmetric double-well potentials, with the energy tracked through training. Presented at SRMIST Research Day 2024.

3.5  Nano & Molecular Simulation Lab, SRMIST · ongoing

Antiviral molecules on doped graphyne nanotubes

Where, and how strongly, does a drug molecule bind to a carbon nanotube once it is doped with boron and zinc?

Live figure. Ribavirin (C8H12N4O5) beside a B/Zn-co-doped γ-graphyne nanotube: one of the input geometries of my QuantumATK study (173 atoms), drawn from the actual coordinates. Drag to rotate.

Problem

Carbon nanocarriers could deliver antivirals such as Ribavirin and Favipiravir, but pristine carbon binds them weakly. Dopants change the local chemistry.

Method

DFT (QuantumATK) of adsorption on B-, N- and Zn-doped γ-graphyne nanotubes at several sites and orientations: adsorption energies, charge transfer, density of states, ELF and QTAIM bond analysis.

Status

Ongoing: ranking dopant sites by binding strength and reversibility.
